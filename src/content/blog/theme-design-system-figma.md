If you're an ecommerce person who isn't a designer, this is the post I'd want you to read first. Because the way you set up your design foundation decides whether AI becomes your design team or just another toy.

## Start with a base theme

Whatever platform you're on, start by finding a base theme. Not one that represents your brand perfectly today. One that's flexible enough to accomplish everything you have in mind as an ecommerce goal, or a digital goal in general, especially if it's conversion related.

That flexibility matters more than how it looks out of the box. You're going to build on it for a long time.

## Build it out as a component library

Then get that theme built out correctly as a component library. Every button, card, section and template, defined once and reused everywhere.

And focus on the style guide and design system right at the beginning. I can't hammer this in enough. This is the part people skip because it doesn't feel like progress. It's the part that makes everything after it possible.

## Connect AI to it

Once the foundation is there, connect Claude to Figma through the Figma MCP, alongside whatever other AI tools you use. Give it your rules: the design system, the component library, the things your AI has to follow.

At that point, if you're willing to just speak, you've put yourself in a position where you've almost effectively replaced the role of a UI/UX designer.

## Then you can start with simple things

Say you want to update your navigation. You can just ask:

- Let's do an analysis of my current site navigation and its UI/UX.
- Now let's look at what the leading competitors, the leading players in this space, are doing.
- Let's rifle through what those options are.

Claude, connected to Figma, is great at getting all of those options in front of you to choose from. You pick, you refine, and you go bit by bit, building and building and building.

The whole time, you keep Claude in compliance with your rules. It follows your design system and your component library, and everything stays linked up. Nothing drifts off-brand, because the system is the source of truth, not the AI's taste.

## Why the foundation pays off

This is where it gets good. Creating clickable prototypes, and going from where you are to dev, becomes very, very easy, because you started with a theme and built the system on top of it.

You've spent your time acting like a true, experienced ecommerce veteran, just by speaking with Claude a little bit, because you took the time to set up your foundation right.
