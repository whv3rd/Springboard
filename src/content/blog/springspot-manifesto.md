Most ecommerce businesses I've worked with run on a stack of dependencies. An agency for the theme. A freelancer for emails. A developer on retainer for anything that touches the catalog. A designer who is booked three weeks out. Every change, however small, goes into somebody else's queue.

That model made sense when the work required specialists for every layer. It makes less sense now.

## What changed

AI models can now work directly inside the tools a store already runs on. Through MCPs (Model Context Protocol servers), Claude can read and update a Shopify catalog, build segments in Klaviyo, and produce pages from a Figma design system. The operator stays in charge. The AI does the repetitive, specialist-shaped work in between.

That doesn't replace good people. It changes what one good person can do.

## The threshold

**Springspot** is the name I use for the point where this actually works: where a business has its design, platform and marketing systems connected to AI, so one operator or a small team can run what used to need a roster of vendors.

It isn't a product you buy. It's a threshold you reach. Some businesses are close. Most are further away than they think, usually because the foundations aren't in place: no real design system, a catalog nobody trusts, processes that live in one person's head.

## What this site is for

I'm building to Springspot at my own brands now, and I'm going to document it here:

- **What Springspot is** and the five checkpoints that define it.
- **The Springspot Score**, a quick way to see where you stand.
- **The Path**, a step-by-step route from wherever you are to the threshold.

The promise is simple: less vendor dependence, faster execution, and the same platforms you already use. No replatforming, no hype.

If you want to know where you stand today, start with the Score.
