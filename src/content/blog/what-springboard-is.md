Every few months a new tool promises to "bring AI to your store." Most of them add a feature. Very few change how the work gets done.

Springboard isn't another tool. It's a description of a state your business is either in or not in.

## The definition

A business has reached Springboard when its **design**, **platform** and **marketing** systems are connected to AI through MCPs, and its core processes run through those connections. At that point, one operator or a small team can do what used to take dev teams, merchandisers and creatives.

## Why "threshold" matters

If Springboard were a product, the question would be "which one should I buy?" Because it's a threshold, the question is "what's missing?"

That shifts the work from shopping to building foundations:

- A design system an AI can actually follow.
- A catalog that is clean enough to be queried and changed safely.
- Marketing data structured well enough to segment from a prompt.
- Processes written down so anyone, human or AI, can run them.

None of those are new ideas. What's new is the payoff. Each one used to be nice to have. Now each one is a prerequisite for letting AI do real work in your business.

## Same platforms, different leverage

You don't need to replatform. Springboard has been proven on Shopify first, with Klaviyo for marketing and Figma for design, and the same thinking applies to other platforms as their MCP support matures.

The goal isn't to buy more software. It's to get more out of what you already pay for.
