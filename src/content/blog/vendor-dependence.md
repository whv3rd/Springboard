When ecommerce teams talk about the cost of agencies and freelancers, they usually talk about invoices. The bigger cost is time.

## The queue is the cost

A homepage update waits for the designer. A bulk product fix waits for the developer. A new segment waits for the email specialist. None of these is expensive on its own. Together, they set the pace of the whole business.

When every change goes through someone else's queue, the business can only move as fast as its slowest dependency. Ideas get batched. Small fixes get skipped. Seasonal work starts late.

## Why it compounds

Dependence also shapes what a team tries. If a test needs three vendors, it doesn't get run. If a landing page takes two weeks, the campaign gets a generic one. Over time, the team stops asking for things that would have made a difference.

## What changes at Springboard

The point of Springboard isn't to cut every vendor. Good specialists are still worth paying for, especially for strategy and hard problems.

The point is that routine, specialist-shaped work stops needing a handoff. When design, platform and marketing are connected to AI, the operator can make the change, on-system, and move on. Vendors get used for what they're best at, not for every small request.

## A useful test

List the last ten changes your team wanted to make to the site, catalog or email program. For each, note who had to be involved and how long it took to go live.

If most of that time was waiting, you're paying for dependence. The five checkpoints are the route out, and the Score will tell you which one to start with.
