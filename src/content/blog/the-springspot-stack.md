A number Mark Cuban likes to repeat: there are about 33 million businesses in the US, and most of them are one person. Most of those people want help putting AI to work, and almost none of them can hire a team to do it.

<!-- WILL: The SBA's Office of Advocacy is the source for ~33 million small businesses, most with no employees. Want to link it, or keep it as the Cuban line? -->

For the ones that sell online, that's who Springspot is for. And the first thing I want them to know is how little it takes to start.

## It's not our stack. It's yours.

Springspot isn't software you rent from me. It's a standard your stack meets: design, platform and marketing connected to AI, with the core processes running through those connections. You own every account. You own every connection. If you stopped reading this blog tomorrow, everything would keep working.

When your stack reaches the top level on the [Springspot Score](/score), it's Springspot-ready. That's the seal. It doesn't come from a vendor; it comes from what your stack can actually do.

## The Springspot Stack

The Springspot Stack covers every job a store runs on:

- **An AI operator and developer** that does the specialist work: pages, catalog fixes, campaigns.
- **A design system**, so every page and asset is on-brand without a designer on call.
- **The store and catalog**: storefront, checkout and products, on a platform you own.
- **Email and SMS automation**: flows and campaigns that run on their own.
- **Reviews and social proof**, collected and shown automatically.

All of it, connected to AI, runs for roughly $100 a month to start. You pay the tool vendors directly, and you own every account.

## Your tools, not our favourites

There's no fixed shopping list. If you already have tools that do these jobs, bring them. Where something is missing, we layer in a new tool based on your business requirements, not on what we like, what you like, or what somebody else likes. We look at what's right for your business, then build the stack around it.

That analysis and setup is the [Springspot Startup](/startup): the package that gets your stack chosen, connected and running.

What's left is a set of processes: how a new product goes from idea to live page, how a campaign gets built, how reviews get asked for. That's what the [Path](/path) teaches, and it costs nothing to run once it's written down.

## What you don't need

For most of my career I worked inside enterprise stacks. Salesforce Commerce Cloud. Adobe Experience Manager. Platforms that are typically licensed in the six figures a year, before you pay the developers and agencies who know how to use them.

<!-- WILL: Add a real number from your own experience here if you can share one, e.g. what a Commerce Cloud or AEM contract looked like. -->

Those platforms exist for good reasons: big catalogs, big teams, big compliance needs. A one-person store has none of those. It needs a site that looks like the brand, email that goes out on its own, and reviews that get collected. Tools that cost tens of dollars a month do all three, and AI does the specialist work in between: building pages from the design system, fixing the catalog, writing and scheduling the campaign.

## Start with the minimum, then automate

The order matters. Start on the cheapest plan of each tool. Connect them to AI. Automate the routine work before you add anything else.

Then scale what you already have. Every tool in the Springspot Stack has bigger plans: more seats, bigger lists, more apps. You move up a plan when revenue or volume asks for it, not before, and you never have to replatform to do it. The processes you automated on day one keep working.

## Cheap stacks make cheap iterations

The real advantage of a small, connected stack isn't the monthly bill. It's how cheap it makes changing your mind.

When a new landing page is a conversation with AI instead of a ticket for a developer, you try more things. You test the offer. You rewrite the product page. You learn faster than you could ship on an enterprise stack.

Sometimes the iteration happens before you even launch. You're learning as you build, and the plan in your head gets better before anyone sees it. That's not a delay; that's the point. We reserve the right to get smarter along the way, and a stack that costs $100 a month lets you.
