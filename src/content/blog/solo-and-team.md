Springboard isn't only for one-person businesses. It shows up in two shapes, and the difference is mostly about who does the work and how much coordination there is.

## Small: a single operator

The solo owner-entrepreneur. One person runs the store, the marketing and the operations, usually with a few freelancers for the parts they can't cover.

For this business, Springboard means fewer freelancers and less waiting. The operator can go from idea to live change in one sitting, because design, catalog and email are all within reach through the same AI.

The biggest risk here is undocumented process. When everything lives in one head, it's hard to hand anything to an AI, or to anyone else.

## Medium: a director plus a couple of operators

A mid-sized business with a director of ecommerce and a small team. This is the shape that used to need an agency roster, a dev team and in-house creatives.

For this business, Springboard means the team spends its time deciding and reviewing rather than briefing and waiting. Work that used to be a ticket becomes something an operator can do directly, on-system, with the director setting the standards.

The biggest risk here is inconsistency. If each operator uses AI their own way, quality drifts. That's why the design system and documented processes matter even more at this size.

## Same checkpoints, different emphasis

Both shapes go through the same five checkpoints. The Score works the same way for both. What changes is where the time goes: a solo operator usually needs the most help with processes, and a small team usually needs the most help with standards.
