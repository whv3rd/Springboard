None of this is the build itself. It's what you need to hand so you don't stop halfway through.

## Accounts and tools

- A Figma account on a plan that lets you keep a shared library and use it across files. Check Figma's current plans before you choose; what each one includes changes.
- Your store platform, with the theme you'll build on, and a safe copy or preview of it you can change without touching the live store.
- Admin access to the theme's settings, so you can see how its sections and blocks are actually put together.

## Source material

- Brand assets: logo files, fonts (with licences that cover web use), colour values and photography.
- Whatever brand guide exists today, even if it's out of date. It's a starting point, not the answer.
- Screenshots of every page template the store runs on now, at desktop and mobile widths.
- Your analytics, so you know which templates get the most traffic and can build those first.

## People and time

- **Solo:** protected time in blocks. This work doesn't happen in the gaps between orders.
- **Team:** one owner for the system. Others can contribute, but one person decides.
- Whoever builds and maintains the theme (you, a developer or an agency) agreeing to work from the system once it exists.

## Decisions to make first

- **Base theme.** Keep the theme you have, or move to a more flexible one before you build the system around it.
- **Scope of the first pass.** Every template eventually. The most-visited ones first.
- **Naming.** One naming convention for variables and components, agreed before anything is built. People and AI both rely on it.
- **Source of truth.** When Figma and the live theme disagree, which one wins. For Springboard, it's Figma, kept in step with the theme.
- **Modes and variants.** Whether you need more than one mode or brand now, such as dark mode, a second store or another market, or whether that can wait.
- **Ownership.** Who approves a change to the system, and how a change gets from Figma to the live theme.
