You can start writing workflows down today. Running them through AI needs the other checkpoints in place first.

## Before you start

- To write workflows down: nothing but time and the people who do the work.
- To run them through AI: the tools each workflow touches connected, ideally at a 3 or better. That usually means the [platform](/path/platform-mcp/overview) and [marketing](/path/marketing-mcp/overview) tracks.

## Tools

- One shared place for your process maps that everyone who runs the store can open.
- The connections from the other tracks: Figma, your store platform and your marketing platform.
- A Claude plan that lets you connect MCP servers, and a way to keep shared instructions and context that every operator works from.
- Access to the places your reporting comes from, such as your store, your email platform, your analytics and your ad accounts.

## Information

- A list of your recurring workflows, with how often each one runs and who runs it today.
- For each one, what it produces: a live product, a sent campaign, a report.
- The tools and logins each one touches.
- Where each one tends to go wrong.

## People and time

- Time with whoever does each workflow today, while they do it. That's where the steps nobody wrote down come out.
- A second person, or a fresh pair of eyes, to test a map by following it.
- One owner for the maps, who updates them when a tool or a process changes.

## Decisions to make first

- **Which workflows are core.** Start with the few the store can't run without.
- **What stays human.** Approvals, judgement calls, anything that spends money or talks to customers. Decide where the human steps are before you automate around them.
- **What "the same result" means.** For each workflow, what done looks like, so you can tell whether anyone, or Claude, got it right.
- **Where outputs land**, and who gets told when a workflow has run.
- **Upkeep.** Who updates a map when the tools change.
